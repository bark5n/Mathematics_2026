### Essential Formulas

**Canonical Equations of Second-Degree Curves (Conics):**
1. **Circle:**
   $$
   x^2 + y^2 = r^2
   $$
   Center at the origin $(0,0)$ and radius $r > 0$.

2. **Ellipse:**
   $$
   \frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1
   $$
   Centered at $(0,0)$ with semiaxes $a$ (along the $x$-axis) and $b$ (along the $y$-axis).

3. **Parabola:**
   $$
   y^2 = 4px \quad (\text{or } x^2 = 4py)
   $$
   Vertex at the origin, symmetric along the coordinate axis, opening in the direction determined by the sign of $p$.

4. **Hyperbola:**
   $$
   \frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1
   $$
   Centered at $(0,0)$, opening along the $x$-axis with vertices at $(\pm a, 0)$ and asymptotes $y = \pm \frac{b}{a}x$.

### Problem Statement

**Exercise 1. Recognizing Curves from Canonical Form**

Recognize the curves

$$
x^2+y^2=9,
$$

$$
\frac{x^2}{16}+\frac{y^2}{9}=1,
$$

$$
y^2=8x,
$$

$$
\frac{x^2}{9}-\frac{y^2}{4}=1.
$$

For each one, identify the most important features and make a sketch. Explain which parts of the equation let you recognize the type of curve[cite: 3].

> **Why this exercise:** builds the basic dictionary: canonical equation → type of curve → shape and scale[cite: 3].

---

### Solution

**Step 1: Analyze the first equation ($x^2 + y^2 = 9$)**
* **Type of curve:** Circle[cite: 3].
* **Features:** The sum of squared variables with equal positive coefficients ($1$ and $1$) equals a positive constant ($9 = 3^2$). The center is at $(0, 0)$ and the radius is $r = \sqrt{9} = 3$.
* **Recognition keys:** Both $x^2$ and $y^2$ appear with identical positive coefficients.

**Step 2: Analyze the second equation ($\frac{x^2}{16} + \frac{y^2}{9} = 1$)**
* **Type of curve:** Ellipse[cite: 3].
* **Features:** The sum of squared variables divided by positive constants equals $1$. The semiaxes are $a = \sqrt{16} = 4$ along the $x$-axis and $b = \sqrt{9} = 3$ along the $y$-axis. The intercepts with the coordinate axes are $(\pm 4, 0)$ and $(0, \pm 3)$.
* **Recognition keys:** Both terms are positive with different denominators under $x^2$ and $y^2$, summing to $1$.

**Step 3: Analyze the third equation ($y^2 = 8x$)**
* **Type of curve:** Parabola[cite: 3].
* **Features:** One variable is squared ($y^2$) while the other appears to the first power ($x$). Here, $4p = 8 \implies p = 2$. The vertex is at the origin $(0,0)$, the axis of symmetry is the $x$-axis, and it opens to the right.
* **Recognition keys:** Exactly one variable is squared, and the linear variable determines the axis and opening direction.

**Step 4: Analyze the fourth equation ($\frac{x^2}{9} - \frac{y^2}{4} = 1$)**
* **Type of curve:** Hyperbola[cite: 3].
* **Features:** The difference of two squared terms equals $1$. The transverse axis is along the $x$-axis with vertices at $(\pm 3, 0)$. The asymptotes are given by $y = \pm \frac{b}{a}x = \pm \frac{2}{3}x$.
* **Recognition keys:** A minus sign between two squared terms with a right-hand side of $1$.

---

### Final Answer

1. **$x^2 + y^2 = 9$:** Circle centered at $(0,0)$ with radius $r = 3$[cite: 3].
2. **$\frac{x^2}{16} + \frac{y^2}{9} = 1$:** Ellipse centered at $(0,0)$ with semiaxes $a = 4$ and $b = 3$[cite: 3].
3. **$y^2 = 8x$:** Parabola with vertex at $(0,0)$, axis along the $x$-axis, opening to the right[cite: 3].
4. **$\frac{x^2}{9} - \frac{y^2}{4} = 1$:** Hyperbola centered at $(0,0)$ with vertices at $(\pm 3, 0)$ and asymptotes $y = \pm \frac{2}{3}x$[cite: 3].
