### Essential Formulas

**Canonical Equation of an Ellipse:**
An ellipse centered at the origin is described by the equation:

$$
\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1
$$

where $a$ and $b$ are the lengths of the semiaxes along the $x$-axis and $y$-axis, respectively.

---

### Problem Statement

**Exercise 3. Ellipse**

For

$$
\frac{x^2}{25}+\frac{y^2}{9}=1,
$$

find the semiaxis lengths, the intercepts with the coordinate axes, and make a sketch. Explain along which axis the ellipse is more stretched and how this is visible in the equation[cite: 3].

> **Why this exercise:** teaches how to read the size and orientation of an ellipse without additional conceptual machinery[cite: 3].

---

### Solution

**Step 1: Determine the semiaxis lengths**

Comparing the given equation $\frac{x^2}{25} + \frac{y^2}{9} = 1$ to the canonical form $\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$:
* $a^2 = 25 \implies a = \sqrt{25} = 5$ (semiaxis along the $x$-axis)
* $b^2 = 9 \implies b = \sqrt{9} = 3$ (semiaxis along the $y$-axis)

**Step 2: Find the intercepts with the coordinate axes**

* **$x$-axis intercepts:** Set $y = 0$:

$$
\frac{x^2}{25} = 1 \implies x^2 = 25 \implies x = \pm 5
$$

  The intercepts with the $x$-axis are $(\pm 5, 0)$.

* **$y$-axis intercepts:** Set $x = 0$:

$$
\frac{y^2}{9} = 1 \implies y^2 = 9 \implies y = \pm 3
$$

  The intercepts with the $y$-axis are $(0, \pm 3)$.

**Step 3: Analyze stretching and orientation**

* **Stretching axis:** Since $a = 5$ is greater than $b = 3$ ($5 > 3$), the ellipse is more stretched along the $x$-axis (horizontal stretch).
* **Visibility in the equation:** The larger denominator is associated with the $x^2$ term ($25 > 9$). In general, the variable with the larger denominator under its squared term dictates the major axis of the ellipse.

---

### Final Answer

1. **Semiaxis lengths:** $a = 5$ (along the $x$-axis) and $b = 3$ (along the $y$-axis)[cite: 3].
2. **Coordinate intercepts:** $(\pm 5, 0)$ on the $x$-axis and $(0, \pm 3)$ on the $y$-axis[cite: 3].
3. **Stretching:** The ellipse is more stretched along the $x$-axis because the denominator of the $x^2$ term is larger than that of the $y^2$ term ($25 > 9$)[cite: 3].
