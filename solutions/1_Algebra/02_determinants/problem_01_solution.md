**Result:**

$$
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
3 & -2 \\
5 & 4 \\
\end{pmatrix}
= 22, \qquad
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 7 \\
2 & 14 \\
\end{pmatrix}
= 0
$$

The first matrix is invertible because its determinant is nonzero. The second is not.

**Steps & Detailed Explanation:**

For a $2 \times 2$ matrix, the determinant is the product of the main diagonal minus the product of the other diagonal:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
3 & -2 \\
5 & 4 \\
\end{pmatrix}
&= (3)(4) - (-2)(5) \\
&= 12 + 10 \\
&= 22
\end{aligned}
$$

For the second matrix:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 7 \\
2 & 14 \\
\end{pmatrix}
&= (1)(14) - (7)(2) \\
&= 14 - 14 \\
&= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

A matrix is invertible if and only if its determinant is nonzero. Therefore, the first matrix is invertible and the second is not.
